## Shared story across all three events

Web Weekend Kathmandu began as a community-first international web conference connecting Nepal's growing technology industry with practitioners from around the world. Its recurring format combined a main conference day with informal community experiences such as a mixer, after-party, collaborative art, and a Kathmandu Valley hike.

The original mission was to break down silos between developers, designers, operations, product people, founders, researchers, and other technologists; encourage storytelling; create partnerships and learning groups; and give Nepal's mature but under-recognized technology industry an international platform.

In 2026 the event evolved from “building the web” to “building with and for AI.” The core values remained consistent: inclusive, community-first, practical, internationally connected, and premium but approachable.

Recurring themes:

- Local and international speakers
- Practical technical talks
- Cross-disciplinary participation
- Community-led learning and networking
- A Kathmandu conference followed by a nature experience
- Access initiatives such as diversity, discounted, volunteer, and community-supported tickets
- Sponsors and community partners from Nepal and abroad

## Core facts

- Dates: September 22–23, 2018
- Location: Kathmandu, Nepal
- Main venue: Bougainvilla Events, Tripureshwor
- Venue contact listed: 01-4260236 / 9851156929
- Format: conference plus community hike and collaborative art
- Status: completed / sold out

## Positioning and mission

The 2018 site describes Nepal's technology industry as a steadily growing ecosystem with experienced developers, active communities, promising startups, and stories that deserved wider recognition.

The event aimed to:

- Connect web technologists from around the world
- Connect Nepal's local technology ecosystem with international practitioners
- Encourage partnerships, collaborations, and learning groups
- Bridge gaps between design, development, and other technology disciplines
- Give developers and designers access to each other's perspectives and challenges
- Combine global storytelling with informal relationship-building

The site highlighted the breadth of Nepal's technology work, from local agriculture to global Hollywood-related projects.

# AI Conf 2026

## Core facts

- Dates: January 10–11, 2026
- Main venue: The Plaza, Kathmandu, Nepal
- Format: two days
  - Day 1: talks, panel discussion, networking/mixer
  - Day 2: community hike
- Status: sold out
- Positioning: Nepal's premier conference for people building the future with AI
- Described as the first major AI conference in Nepal
- Audience: engineers, designers, product people, founders, researchers, and builders creating real AI-powered products, interfaces, and systems

## Transition in the WWKTM story

Key positioning statement:

> From building the web to building with & for AI. This is the next chapter of Web Weekend Kathmandu.

The event retained WWKTM's inclusive, community-first approach while moving the subject matter toward applied AI.

## Highlighted themes

- Security and AI
- Enterprise RAG
- LLM system architecture
- Observability and MLOps
- Human-in-the-loop AI
- Agents and tool use
- AI design leadership
- Responsible and humanity-first AI
- AI understanding and agency
- Programming languages for AI-assisted development

## Main speakers and talks

1. Amit Timalsina — AI Consultant; founder of Blintic AI and Algentech
   - Talk: “Automating Clinical Trial Reviews using Generative AI”
   - Focus: processing 120,000+ complex documents; approximately $2 per document; preserving layout and structure; extracting difficult tables; citation tracking; verifiability; AWS Bedrock throughput; model trade-offs; production AI in a regulated environment.

2. Vlad Dyachenko — Platform Engineer and open-source contributor
   - Talk: “The Perfect Programming Language for the AI Era: How Should It Look?”
   - Focus: language design for people and AI agents; Rust, Python, Go, and Ruby; expressiveness, safety, performance, token economics, compiler feedback, traits, macros, and extensibility.

3. Sayantika Banik — Founder of DataJourneyHQ; open-source maintainer
   - Talk: “The Art & Science of Designing LLM Systems”
   - Focus: trustworthy LLM systems; security, explainability, reproducibility, governance, telemetry, guardrails, and keeping humans involved in decisions.

4. Dijup Tuladhar — Senior Principal Product Designer at Infinite; founder of Chordpedia
   - Talk: “AI Is Redefining Design Leadership: From Execution to Strategic Decision-Making”
   - Focus: designers moving from production execution to direction and strategy; Figma, Figma Make, Copilot, research synthesis, exploration, design operations, collaboration, and AI as a force multiplier.

5. Manu Chatterjee — VP and Head of AI at Leapfrog Technology
   - Talk: “What AI Actually Does: From Unstructured Chaos to Intelligent Systems”
   - Focus: how LLMs transform unstructured input into decisions, actions, and content; reasoning, planning, workflow design, hallucinations, intent-to-output systems, and practical AI architecture.

6. Saugat Acharya — Principal Engineer at Laudio; open-source and technology enthusiast
   - Talk: “Evalobility: Why PMs, Designers, and Engineers Now Share the Same Dashboard”
   - Focus: LLM evaluation plus observability; shared traces; experiment monitoring; output quality; cost; performance; UX impact; Datadog LLM Observability; human feedback.

7. Pranjal Timsina — Synapse Technologies and Kanoon AI
   - Talk: “AI & Security”
   - Focus: misuse prevention, harmful outputs, privacy, non-deterministic automation, agent liability, tool-call poisoning, leaked training information, and training-data poisoning.

8. Haihao Liu — ML researcher at Algoverse; founder of Ternity Education
   - Talk: “Building Humanity-First AI for the 22nd Century”
   - Focus: AI safety, children, education, neuroscience, responsible adoption, alignment, uncertainty about how AI works, and encouraging builders to consider long-term human consequences.

9. Dr. Dovan Rai — Director of Research and Development at Body & Data
   - Talk: “Does AI Understand? AI, Understanding, and Agency”
   - Focus: pattern generation versus human understanding; LLM comprehension; emergent behavior; agency; existential-risk debates; digital rights and education.

10. Aayush — full-stack developer, stand-up comedian, and storyteller
   - Listed as part of the speaker/entertainment program.

# Strong historical facts and copy usable for AI Conf 2027

## Proven event lineage

- WWKTM has run major community technology events since 2018.
- The signature conference-plus-hike format dates back to the first Web Weekend Kathmandu.
- The event has consistently hosted national and international speakers.
- Past topics span software engineering, web technologies, infrastructure, product management, design, security, open source, responsible technology, and AI.
- AI Conf 2026 sold out.

## Credibility numbers supported by the archives

- 2018: 10 listed speakers
- 2019: 11 listed speakers plus a host
- 2026: 9 technical talks, one panel, multiple panelists, a host/performer, and a community hike
- The historical archive supports the phrase “a wwktm tradition since 2018” for the community hike.

## Strong proof points

- International speakers have included practitioners connected to Uber, Adobe, Directi, Auth0, Evolution Gaming, GitNation, MIT, Harvard, Algoverse, and global open-source communities.
- Local ecosystem participation includes Leapfrog Technology, CloudFactory, Logpoint, Body & Data, NAAMII, Tangible Careers, Infinite, Synapse Technologies, Kanoon AI, and many others.
- The event has repeatedly supported community access through discounted, community-funded, diversity, and volunteer pathways.

## Strong testimonial themes

- Cross-disciplinary conversations beyond narrow conference categories
- Engaged and knowledgeable young attendees
- A combination of technical content, community, food, nature, and travel
- International-local connection
- The hike and informal time are repeatedly remembered as core parts of the experience
